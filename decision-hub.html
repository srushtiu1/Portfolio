Overview
Decisions were happening everywhere except where they should
Organizations often rely on spreadsheets, presentations, and disconnected reporting tools when evaluating strategic initiatives. This fragmented workflow slows executive decision-making and reduces visibility into portfolio performance.
The goal was to design a centralized workspace where leadership teams could evaluate initiatives, assess risks, compare scenarios, and approve strategic investments from one platform without having to chase context across six different systems.

The Business Problem
Strategic decisions were fragmented across five different tools
Executives received information from multiple disconnected systems. Investment requests lacked consistency. Scenario analysis happened in spreadsheets. Approvals occurred across meetings and email. Portfolio visibility was always a week behind.

Product Goals
Five product priorities
Goal 01
Reduce executive decision time
Surface the right request, with the right context, at the right moment, so decisions happen in the platform, not after a meeting.
Goal 02
Centralize strategic initiatives
Replace the fragmented spreadsheet and email workflow with one place where all initiatives live and can be compared.
Goal 03
Improve portfolio visibility
Give leadership a real-time view of performance across all active projects and not a weekly PowerPoint summary.
Goal 04
Enable scenario comparison
Let executives model the impact of different decisions before committing, replacing gut instinct with structured analysis.
Goal 05
Support cross-functional approvals
Bring IT, HR, Finance, and executive leadership into one approval loop, removing the back-and-forth that stretched timelines across weeks.

FEEDBACK & REPORTING
Decisions and outcomes feed back into performance monitoring and portfolio reporting.
Dashboard
Every placement is a decision about priority
The dashboard is not a reporting surface. It is an action surface. Everything on it is there because an executive needs to see it before doing anything else or because it provides context for a decision already in progress.
Decision 01
The alert banner sits above the KPIs because a risk outranks a status
KPI cards show the current state of the business. An alert signals that the current state is about to change or already has. Letting a budget overrun notification sit below a revenue number would mean an executive might celebrate $12.4M while Project Phoenix burns through its contingency. The alert comes first, every time, with a direct path to resolution.
Decision 02
Approvals appear as a primary module, not a notification badge
Approval requests are actions, not context. In the fragmented workflow the platform replaces, approval requests arrived as emails and were discussed in meetings. Elevating them to a primary dashboard module means a Chief Executive opening the platform at 8am sees exactly what needs a decision before the day starts — with the department, budget, and both actions immediately visible. No inbox required.
Decision 03
Performance Trend lives beside decisions, not beneath them
An executive approving a $45k infrastructure expansion needs to see the overall efficiency trend before clicking Approve — not after. Placing the trend chart in the same visual tier as the approval queue means both pieces of information are in view at the same time. The layout is doing the work that a conversation in a meeting used to do.
Constraints & Open Questions
This project didn't ship
The Decision Intelligence Hub was designed through to a complete, internally reviewed product, architecture, dashboard, approval flow, and scenario comparison were all specified and validated with stakeholders but it moved into engineering handoff before priorities shifted, and it didn't go into production. Worth being upfront about that: this is design work I stand behind, not a live product with usage behind it.
Because it didn't ship, there's no accessibility validation or adoption data to report, the accessibility pass in particular is work that typically happens closer to release, and this project didn't get there. What follows describes what the design was built to do, not what it measurably achieved.
Design outcomes
One connected decision loop, not five disconnected tools
The design consolidated a workflow spread across spreadsheets, email, meetings, and decks into a single connected surface where monitoring, evaluation, and approval could happen in one place. Since the project didn't ship, this describes what the design was built to achieve rather than a measured result.
Reflection
Designing for decisions, not just information
This project reinforced how important hierarchy becomes when an interface is built around high-stakes decisions. The challenge wasn't to surface everything an executive might need, but to make the right information visible at the right moment and make the next action obvious.
What Worked
Prioritizing decisions and risks over secondary analytics on the executive dashboard.
Using visual hierarchy and restrained color to distinguish important signals without overwhelming the interface.
Connecting monitoring, analysis, and approval into one continuous decision workflow.
What I'd Improve
Validate the dashboard hierarchy with executive users earlier in the process.
Explore the scenario simulator further, particularly how complex financial parameters are presented and compared.
Extend the approval experience to mobile for decisions that need to happen away from the desktop.
